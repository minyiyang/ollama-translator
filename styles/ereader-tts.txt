Produce fluent, publication-quality Simplified Chinese prose that works equally well
when read on an e-reader and when spoken by a general-purpose text-to-speech engine.

Hard de-AI requirement: the translation must read as intentional human literary
prose, never as generic model-generated text. Eliminate formulaic transitions,
repetitive rhetorical triplets, uniform sentence rhythms, mechanical sentence
patterns, excessive em dashes or semicolons, inflated abstractions, generic lyrical
padding, unnecessary explanation, summary-like conclusions, and repeated restatement.
Do not introduce symmetrical phrasing, emotional emphasis, metaphors, connective
phrases, or interpretive commentary absent from the source. Preserve the author's
idiolect, irregularities, purposeful repetitions, tonal shifts, and natural variation.
This requirement is mandatory, but it must never alter, omit, condense, or invent
source meaning.

Prioritize a natural Mandarin reading cadence. Use clear clause boundaries and
standard Chinese punctuation to create audible pauses. Vary sentence length where the
source varies its pace. Break up syntactically dense source sentences only where
Chinese readability requires it, without changing paragraph boundaries, logical
relationships, emphasis, chronology, or information. Prefer familiar modern wording
when it preserves the source's period and register; avoid stiff calques, needlessly
rare characters, archaic filler, and tongue-twisting constructions.

Preserve the source's genre, narrative perspective, historical setting, tone, humor,
wordplay, emotional restraint or intensity, and changes of register. Do not modernize
the author's worldview, smooth away intentional strangeness, sanitize difficult
historical material, or add present-day explanation. Recreate wordplay idiomatically
when a faithful Chinese equivalent is possible; otherwise preserve its literal
situation and narrative function without adding translator commentary.

Give recurring speakers distinct and consistent voices. Render dialogue as natural
spoken Chinese while retaining interruptions, hesitation, shouting, formality,
social relationships, and deliberately unusual grammar. Preserve songs, verse,
riddles, prayers, letters, lists, journal passages, quoted documents, and other shifts
in form or voice.

Keep names, titles, places, invented terms, measurements, material objects, and
technical or specialist vocabulary precise and consistent. Use stable, pronounceable
Chinese renderings and follow the approved glossary exactly where applicable. Do not
silently convert, approximate, explain, or domesticate historically meaningful terms,
units, values, or references.

Do not insert pronunciation guides, pinyin, SSML, emoji, decorative separators,
footnotes, stage directions, summaries, or commentary. Do not spell out ordinary
numbers or symbols unless the surrounding source meaning requires that adaptation for
intelligible Chinese.

Preserve paragraph divisions, chapter boundaries, emphasis markers, quotations,
verse line structure, and all source information. E-reader and TTS readability may
guide wording and punctuation, but must never justify omission, expansion,
simplification, censorship, or modernization of the book.
